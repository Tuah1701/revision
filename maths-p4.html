What the modelling words mean in your equations

WordSo in the equations…
ParticleAll mass at a point: no rotation, no size, forces all act at one point.
SmoothNo friction: \(F = 0\). Only \(R\) acts at the contact.
RoughFriction acts, \(F \le \mu R\).
Limiting equilibrium / about to slip\(F = \mu R\) exactly, and acceleration is still 0.
Light (string, rod, pulley)Mass ignored, so tension is the same all the way along.
InextensibleDoesn't stretch: connected particles share the same magnitude of acceleration and speed.
Smooth pulleyTension the same on both sides; only the direction changes.
Rigid rod / tow-barCan be in tension (pulling) or thrust (pushing). A string can only be in tension.
CoalesceThe two bodies stick together and move with one common velocity.

Trap"Limiting" doesn't mean moving. It means on the point of moving, so \(a = 0\) and \(F = \mu R\).
1 · Forces & equilibriumConcept

Resolve everything into two perpendicular directions

Components θ from the x-axis

\(F_x = F\cos\theta, \qquad F_y = F\sin\theta\)

Resultant of several forces

\(X = \sum F_x,\ \ Y = \sum F_y\), then \(R = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2}\), \(\tan\alpha = \dfrac{Y}{X}\)

  • cos goes with the angle you're touching: the component adjacent to the marked angle uses cos.
  • Give the direction of a resultant as an angle from a stated line ("8.66° below the horizontal").
  • Two forces in equilibrium are equal and opposite. Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle of forces (sine/cosine rule works).
Quick checkDraw a rough sketch of \(X\) and \(Y\) with their signs before you take \(\tan^{-1}\). The calculator only gives \(-90°\) to \(90°\).
1 · Forces & equilibriumConcept

Equilibrium: resultant force is zero

At rest or moving with constant velocity (Newton's first law).

Σ(components in any direction) = 0

Choosing directions

Flat ground

Resolve horizontally and vertically.

Slope

Resolve parallel and perpendicular to the slope. Only the weight (and any horizontal force) needs splitting.

Unknown you don't want

Resolve perpendicular to it and it disappears from that equation (the note's tugboat example: resolve perpendicular to \(T\)).

Newton's third lawIf A pushes on B, B pushes back on A with an equal and opposite force. That's why the floor pushes up on you (\(R\)) and why tension acts towards the centre of the string on both connected bodies.
Trap\(R\) is not always \(mg\). Any force with a vertical component (a pull at an angle, a boy pushing down on a chair) changes \(R\), and therefore changes \(\mu R\).
1 · Forces & equilibriumConcept

Friction: a force that grows until it can't

Friction law

\(0 \le F \le \mu R\). At limiting equilibrium or when sliding, \(F = \mu R\).

  • Friction only takes the value it needs to stop motion, up to the maximum \(\mu R\).
  • Friction opposes the motion or the motion that would happen (the tendency). Decide which way it would slide before you draw \(F\).
  • Smooth means \(\mu = 0\). Usually \(0 \le \mu \le 1\), but \(\mu\) can exceed 1 (tutorial Q7 uses \(\mu = 1.125\)).
  • Angle of friction: \(\tan\lambda = \mu\). On a slope with no other force, the particle is on the point of slipping when \(\tan\theta = \mu\).
Applied pushResult
Less than \(\mu R\)Stays at rest, \(F\) = push
Equal to \(\mu R\)Limiting equilibrium, about to slip
Greater than \(\mu R\)Moves; \(F = \mu R\) and use \(F_{\text{res}} = ma\)
1 · Forces & equilibriumConcept

Rough slopes: two critical cases

About to slip DOWN

Friction acts up the slope. Gives the least \(P\):
\(P + \mu R = mg\sin\theta\)

About to move UP

Friction acts down the slope. Gives the greatest \(P\):
\(P = mg\sin\theta + \mu R\)

For any \(P\) between these two values, the particle stays at rest. Answer "range of values" as \(P_{\min} \le P \le P_{\max}\).

TrapIf \(P\) acts at an angle to the slope, \(R\) is different in the two cases' equations only if you forget it. \(R = mg\cos\theta - P\sin\alpha\) depends on \(P\), so substitute it in before solving for \(P\).

The tractor: resultant of two forces

Q (Forces note): Two people push a tractor with forces 210 N at 15° above the horizontal and 190 N at 35° below the horizontal, in the same vertical plane. Find the resultant.

  1. Diagram, directions: both forces point forward; take right (forward) and up as positive.
  2. M1 Resolve horizontally: \(X = 210\cos15° + 190\cos35° = 202.84 + 155.64 = 358.48\ \text{N}\)
  3. M1 Resolve vertically: \(Y = 210\sin15° - 190\sin35° = 54.35 - 108.98 = -54.63\ \text{N}\) (downward)
  4. M1 \(R = \sqrt{358.48^2 + 54.63^2} = 363\ \text{N}\) A1
  5. \(\tan\alpha = \dfrac{54.63}{358.48}\Rightarrow \alpha = 8.66°\) below the horizontal A1
Why it worksKeep 5 s.f. in the working and round only at the end, or the angle drifts to 8.7°.
Worked example · 1 · Forces & equilibriumWorked example

Least P to stop slipping, least P to move it up

Q (Forces note): A block of weight 30 N is on a rough plane inclined at \(\theta\), \(\tan\theta = \frac34\). A force \(P\) N acts up the slope, parallel to it. \(\mu = \frac13\). Find the least \(P\) that (a) prevents slipping down, (b) makes it move up.

  1. \(\tan\theta = \frac34 \Rightarrow \sin\theta = 0.6,\ \cos\theta = 0.8\) (3-4-5 triangle).
  2. M1 Perpendicular to slope: \(R = 30\cos\theta = 24\ \text{N}\), so \(F_{\max} = \frac13(24) = 8\ \text{N}\). Weight component down slope: \(30\sin\theta = 18\ \text{N}\).
  3. M1 (a) About to slip down: friction acts up. Parallel: \(P + 8 = 18\), so \(P = 10\ \text{N}\) A1
  4. M1 (b) About to move up: friction acts down. Parallel: \(P = 18 + 8 = 26\ \text{N}\) A1
  5. So the block stays at rest for \(10 \le P \le 26\).
Where marks goFriction flips direction between parts. Same magnitude, opposite sign.
2 · KinematicsConcept

Constant acceleration: SUVAT

EquationMissing
\(v = u + at\)\(s\)
\(s = \frac12(u+v)t\)\(a\)
\(s = ut + \frac12at^2\)\(v\)
\(s = vt - \frac12at^2\)\(u\)
\(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\)\(t\)
  • List \(s, u, v, a, t\); circle the 3 you know and the 1 you want; pick the equation missing the 5th.
  • Displacement, velocity, acceleration are vectors (sign matters). Distance and speed are scalars.
  • Average velocity = net displacement ÷ total time. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
Only for constant aIf \(a\) depends on \(t\) (e.g. \(v = 0.6t^2 - 0.12t^3\)), SUVAT is wrong. Use calculus.
Two unknowns?Write two SUVAT equations for two stages from the same start point (e.g. A→B and A→C) and solve simultaneously. Quiz 1 Q1 and Assignment Q4 both do this.
2 · KinematicsConcept

Vertical motion under gravity

Ignoring air resistance, everything moves with \(a = g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) downward, whatever its mass.

  • Choose up positive for a thrown-up object: \(u > 0\), \(a = -10\).
  • At the greatest height, \(v = 0\). Back at the start, \(s = 0\).
  • \(s = 25\) on the way up and on the way down gives two values of \(t\): the quadratic's two roots.
  • Two balls colliding: write \(s\) for each with its own start time (ball thrown 1 s later uses \(t - 1\)), then set positions equal.
From the noteBall up at 30 m s⁻¹: greatest height \(0 = 900 - 20s \Rightarrow s = 45\) m; returns after \(0 = 30t - 5t^2 \Rightarrow t = 6\) s; at 25 m when \(5t^2 - 30t + 25 = 0\), so \(t = 1\) or \(5\) s.
TrapMixing signs: if up is positive, a ball dropped from 30 m ends at \(s = -30\), not \(+30\).
2 · KinematicsConcept

Reading s–t and v–t graphs

Displacement–time

  • Gradient = velocity
  • Horizontal line = at rest
  • Curve = changing velocity

Velocity–time

  • Gradient = acceleration
  • Area under = displacement
  • Below the axis = moving backwards
Distance vs displacementIf the v–t graph goes below the axis, distance = sum of the areas (all positive), displacement = area above − area below.
Trapezium shortcutFor accelerate–cruise–decelerate, area = \(\frac12(\text{top} + \text{bottom}) \times v\). With total distance given, this is often one equation in one unknown.
2 · KinematicsConcept

Variable acceleration: use calculus

\(s\)→ d/dt →\(v\)→ d/dt →\(a\)
\(a\)→ ∫ dt →\(v\)→ ∫ dt →\(s\)
  • Every indefinite integral needs \(+c\). Find it from the given condition ("starts from rest" ⇒ \(v = 0\) at \(t = 0\); "from O" ⇒ \(s = 0\) at \(t = 0\)).
  • Instantaneous rest: solve \(v = 0\).
  • Maximum velocity: solve \(a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 0\); justify with \(\frac{d^2v}{dt^2} < 0\).
  • Distance between times: \(\int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt\) — but split the integral wherever \(v\) changes sign and add the magnitudes.
From the noteGo-kart \(v = \frac1{10}t(36 - t)\): \(\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{36 - 2t}{10} = 0 \Rightarrow t = 18\) s, \(v_{\max} = 32.4\ \text{m s}^{-1}\); \(\frac{d^2v}{dt^2} = -\frac15 < 0\), so a maximum.
Worked example · 2 · KinematicsWorked example

Find a and u from two equal-time stages

Q (Kinematics Quiz 1 Q1): P moves along ABCD with constant acceleration. AB = 100 m, BC = 148 m; each takes 4 s. (a) Show \(a = 3\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) and find the speed at A. [6] (b) P reaches D at 61 m s⁻¹. Find CD. [3]

  1. Let \(u\) = speed at A. Use \(s = ut + \frac12at^2\) from A both times so \(u\) is the same unknown.
  2. M1A1 A→B (\(t = 4\)): \(100 = 4u + 8a\)
  3. M1A1 A→C (\(t = 8\), \(s = 248\)): \(248 = 8u + 32a\)
  4. M1 Double the first: \(200 = 8u + 16a\). Subtract: \(48 = 16a\), so \(a = 3\) A1 (shown), then \(u = 19\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) A1
  5. B1 (b) Speed at C: \(v = 19 + 3(8) = 43\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
  6. M1 \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\): \(61^2 = 43^2 + 6s \Rightarrow s = \dfrac{3721 - 1849}{6} = 312\ \text{m}\) A1
Common slipUsing B→C with \(u = 0\). The particle is still moving at B.

"Show that OP = 6.25 m"

Q (Kinematics Quiz 1 Q2a): A particle starts from rest at O and comes to instantaneous rest at P. \(v = 0.6t^2 - 0.12t^3\). Show that OP = 6.25 m. [5]

  1. \(v\) is a function of \(t\) ⇒ acceleration not constant ⇒ calculus, not SUVAT.
  2. M1 Rest at P: \(0.6t^2 - 0.12t^3 = t^2(0.6 - 0.12t) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 5\) (t = 0 is O) A1
  3. M1 Integrate: \(s = \int_0^5 (0.6t^2 - 0.12t^3)\,dt = \left[0.2t^3 - 0.03t^4\right]_0^5\) A1
  4. \(= 0.2(125) - 0.03(625) = 25 - 18.75 = 6.25\ \text{m}\) A1 (AG: answer given, so show every line)
"Show that"The answer is given, so the marks are for the working. Show the limits, the substituted values and the subtraction.
Worked example · 2 · KinematicsWorked example

Cyclist and car: total time, then overtaking

Q (Assignment 1 Q1): A cyclist starts from rest at A, accelerates at 0.5 m s⁻² for 36 m, travels at constant speed for 25 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest at B. AB = 210 m. (a) Find the total time. [5] (b) 24 s after the cyclist leaves A, a car starts from rest at A with acceleration 4 m s⁻². It overtakes the cyclist during her constant-speed stage. Find the time from the cyclist's start until the car overtakes. [5]

  1. M1 Stage 1: \(v^2 = 0 + 2(0.5)(36) \Rightarrow v = 6\); \(t = 6 / 0.5 = 12\) s.
  2. B1 Stage 2: \(6 \times 25 = 150\) m. Stage 3 distance: \(210 - 36 - 150 = 24\) m.
  3. M1 Stage 3: \(24 = \frac12(6 + 0)t \Rightarrow t = 8\) s. Total \(= 12 + 25 + 8 = 45\) s A1
  4. M1 (b) Let \(T\) = time since the cyclist started. Cyclist (cruising): \(s = 36 + 6(T - 12)\). Car: \(s = \frac12(4)(T - 24)^2 = 2(T - 24)^2\).
  5. M1 Equal: \(2(T - 24)^2 = 6T - 36 \Rightarrow T^2 - 51T + 594 = 0\) A1
  6. A1 \(T = 18\) or \(33\). \(T = 18\) is before the car starts, so \(T = 33\) s (and 33 is inside the cruise window 12–37 s ✓).
TrapUsing the car's own clock for both. Put both on one time variable and shift the later starter by its delay.
3 · Newton's lawsConcept

Newton's second law: \(F_{\text{res}} = ma\)

  1. First law: no resultant force ⇒ rest or constant velocity.
  2. Second law: resultant force = rate of change of momentum = \(ma\) (constant mass).
  3. Third law: A on B is equal and opposite to B on A.
ForceDirection / size
Weight \(W = mg\)Vertically down. Mass in kg, weight in N.
Normal reaction \(R\)Perpendicular to the surface, away from it.
Friction \(F\)Along the surface, against (attempted) motion; \(\le \mu R\).
Tension \(T\)Along the string, away from the body.
ThrustAlong a rod, towards the body (pushing).
Driving force \(D\)Forward; from an engine (\(D = P/v\)).
ResistanceOpposes motion. "0.5 N per kg" ⇒ \(0.5m\).
Write it in this order(forces in direction of \(a\)) − (forces against) \(= ma\). Then the sign of \(a\) is automatic.
3 · Newton's lawsConcept

Moving on a slope

  • Perpendicular: no acceleration, so \(R = mg\cos\theta\) (± components of any angled force).
  • Parallel: \(F_{\text{res}} = ma\). Friction is now exactly \(\mu R\) because it's sliding.
Sliding down: ma = mg sinθ − μ mg cosθ
Pushed up: ma = P − mg sinθ − μ mg cosθ
Exact trig\(\sin\theta = 0.6 \Rightarrow \cos\theta = 0.8\); \(\sin\theta = \frac{7}{25} \Rightarrow \cos\theta = \frac{24}{25}\); \(\sin\theta = 0.05 \Rightarrow\) usually only \(\sin\theta\) is needed (no friction). Use exact values, not rounded angles.
TrapWhen a particle slides up, stops, then slides back, friction changes direction and so does the acceleration. New stage, new equation.

Lifts

Person in a lift: \(R - mg = ma\) (up positive). Accelerating up or decelerating while moving down ⇒ \(R > mg\). Note: man of 70 kg, lift from rest to 10 m s⁻¹ in 2 s: \(a = 5\), \(R = 70(10 + 5) = 1050\) N.

3 · Newton's lawsConcept

Connected particles: two equations, or one plus one

Whole system

Treat car + trailer as one body. Internal tension cancels. Gives \(a\) quickly.
\(D - R_1 - R_2 = (m_1 + m_2)a\)

Separate body

One particle on its own. Tension appears. Gives \(T\).
Trailer: \(T - R_2 = m_2a\)

Use the system equation to find \(a\), then the simpler single body (usually the one with fewer forces) to find \(T\).

Strings over pulleys

TrapYou can't use a "whole system" equation for a string that changes direction unless you take "along the string" as the direction for each particle. Safer: write \(F = ma\) for each particle separately and add.
After the string goes slackWhen the falling particle hits the floor, \(T = 0\). The other particle continues with its current speed and moves freely (\(a = -g\) if vertical, \(a = -g\sin\theta\) on a smooth slope).

Car towing a trailer

Q (Newton note): Car 700 kg tows a trailer 200 kg on a horizontal road. Driving force 1220 N. Resistances: car 200 N, trailer 300 N. Find the acceleration and the tension in the tow-bar.

  1. Diagram: car: \(1220\) forward, \(200\) back, \(T\) back. Trailer: \(T\) forward, \(300\) back. Positive = direction of motion.
  2. M1 Whole system, \(F = ma\): \(1220 - 200 - 300 = 900a\)
  3. \(a = \dfrac{720}{900} = 0.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) A1
  4. M1 Trailer alone: \(T - 300 = 200(0.8)\)
  5. \(T = 460\ \text{N}\) A1. Check with the car: \(1220 - 200 - 460 = 560 = 700(0.8)\) ✓
Tension or thrust?If your \(T\) comes out negative, the tow-bar is in thrust (pushing). That's the point of the note's caravan-uphill example: say which it is.
Worked example · 3 · Newton's lawsWorked example

Pulley system, then free motion

Q (Newton's 2nd law sheet Q6): A (3 kg) and B (5 kg) hang over a smooth pulley, string taut. A is 1 m above a horizontal plane, B is 2 m above it. Released from rest; B stays on the plane once it lands; A doesn't reach the pulley. (a) Find \(T\) and the time for B to reach the plane. (b) Find the time for which A is at least 3.25 m above the plane.

  1. M1 B (down +): \(50 - T = 5a\). A (up +): \(T - 30 = 3a\).
  2. M1 Add: \(20 = 8a \Rightarrow a = 2.5\ \text{m s}^{-2}\); \(T = 30 + 7.5 = 37.5\ \text{N}\) A1
  3. M1 B falls 2 m: \(2 = \frac12(2.5)t^2 \Rightarrow t = \sqrt{1.6} = 1.26\ \text{s}\) A1
  4. B1 (b) When B lands, A has risen 2 m to 3 m, with \(v^2 = 2(2.5)(2) = 10\), \(v = \sqrt{10}\).
  5. M1 String slack ⇒ A moves freely, \(a = -10\). Height above plane: \(3 + \sqrt{10}\,t - 5t^2 \ge 3.25\)
  6. M1 \(5t^2 - \sqrt{10}\,t + 0.25 = 0\): roots differ by \(\dfrac{\sqrt{10 - 5}}{5} = 0.447\ \text{s}\) A1
ShortcutThe time between the two roots of \(at^2 + bt + c = 0\) is \(\dfrac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{a}\).

Pulley at the bottom of a rough slope

Q (Newton's 2nd law sheet Q7): A (0.2 kg) on a rough plane, \(\sin\theta = 0.6\), \(\mu = 1.125\), is joined over a smooth pulley at the bottom of the plane to B (0.3 kg) hanging 0.25 m above the ground. Released from rest. (a) Find \(T\) and \(a\). (b) B lands and stops. Find the total distance A travels down the plane.

  1. B1 A: \(R = 0.2(10)(0.8) = 1.6\ \text{N}\), so \(F = 1.125(1.6) = 1.8\ \text{N}\), acting up the slope (A moves down).
  2. M1 A (down slope +): \(T + 0.2(10)(0.6) - 1.8 = 0.2a \Rightarrow T + 1.2 - 1.8 = 0.2a\)
  3. M1 B (down +): \(3 - T = 0.3a\). Add: \(2.4 = 0.5a \Rightarrow a = 4.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}\), \(T = 3 - 1.44 = 1.56\ \text{N}\) A1A1
  4. M1 After 0.25 m: \(v^2 = 2(4.8)(0.25) = 2.4\).
  5. M1 String slack: A has \(1.2\) N down slope, \(1.8\) N friction up slope ⇒ deceleration \(= \dfrac{0.6}{0.2} = 3\ \text{m s}^{-2}\).
  6. A1 \(0 = 2.4 - 2(3)s \Rightarrow s = 0.4\) m. Total \(= 0.25 + 0.4 = 0.65\ \text{m}\).

4 · MomentumConcept

Momentum is conserved in a collision

Momentum vector, N s or kg m s⁻¹

\(p = mv\)

Conservation of linear momentum

\(m_Au_A + m_Bu_B = m_Av_A + m_Bv_B\)

Coalesce: \(m_Au_A + m_Bu_B = (m_A + m_B)v\)

  • Pick one positive direction and keep it for every velocity, before and after. Anything moving the other way is negative.
  • Unknown velocity? Leave it as \(+v\); a negative answer just means it moves the other way.
  • Change in momentum of one body = \(m(v - u)\), with signs. Rebounding ball: \(u = -3,\ v = +6\) gives \(\Delta p = 9m\), not \(3m\).
  • Kinetic energy is not usually conserved: loss of KE = KE before − KE after (common follow-up).
  • Chain collisions (A hits B, B hits C): apply conservation to each collision separately.
Combine with kinematicsMomentum questions often need SUVAT first (speed just before impact) and after (where the coalesced object lands). The note's balls X and Y do both.
Worked example · 4 · MomentumWorked example

Opposite directions before impact

Q (Momentum note): P (0.5 kg) and Q (0.2 kg) move towards each other at 8 m s⁻¹ and 2 m s⁻¹. After the collision P's direction is unchanged and Q's speed is double P's. Find (a) P's speed after, (b) the change in Q's momentum.

  1. Direction: P's original direction positive. Before: \(u_P = 8,\ u_Q = -2\). After: \(v_P = v,\ v_Q = 2v\) (Q must now move in P's direction, ahead of it).
  2. M1 CoM: \(0.5(8) + 0.2(-2) = 0.5v + 0.2(2v)\) A1
  3. \(3.6 = 0.9v \Rightarrow v = 4\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) A1
  4. M1 (b) \(\Delta p_Q = 0.2(8 - (-2)) = 2\ \text{N s}\) in P's original direction A1
Biggest momentum errorWriting \(0.2(2)\) instead of \(0.2(-2)\) for Q before impact. Signs come from your chosen direction, not from the word "speed".

Find u when the particles coalesce

Q (Momentum note): A (2 kg) and B (3 kg) move towards each other with speeds \(u\) and \(2u\) m s⁻¹. They coalesce and move in B's original direction at 4 m s⁻¹. Find \(u\).

  1. Direction: B's original direction positive, so A's velocity is \(-u\).

5 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Work and energy

QuantityFormulaNote
Work done by a force\(W = Fs\cos\theta\)\(\theta\) between force and displacement. J.
Kinetic energy\(\frac12mv^2\)Scalar: direction doesn't matter.
Gravitational PE\(mgh\)\(h\) is vertical height; state the zero level.
WD against gravity on a slope\(mg \times (d\sin\theta)\)Only the vertical rise counts.
WD against friction\(\mu R \times d\)Distance along the surface.
Work–energy principle

WD by driving force − WD against resistance
\(= \Delta KE + \Delta PE\)

No resistances and no driving force ⇒ \(KE + PE\) is conserved.

When to use energyWhen the question gives distances or heights but no time, or the resistance isn't constant (only its work is given). Energy can't give you time directly.
TrapUsing the slope length \(d\) for \(h\) in \(mgh\). Height gained = \(d\sin\theta\).
5 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Power: the rate of doing work

Power W = J s⁻¹

\(P = \dfrac{\text{work done}}{\text{time}}\), and for a driving force at speed \(v\): \(P = Dv\)

Given \(P\) and \(v\)→\(D = \dfrac{P}{v}\)→\(D - R - mg\sin\theta = ma\)
  • Maximum / steady speed: \(a = 0\), so \(D = R\) (+ \(mg\sin\theta\) uphill, − downhill). Then \(v_{\max} = P / D\).
  • kW ⇒ ×1000. "Working at 4 kW" means \(P = 4000\).
  • Power constant over a time \(t\) ⇒ work done by the engine \(= Pt\) (use it in the work–energy equation).
Trap\(P = Fv\) uses the driving force, not the resultant force. Find \(D\) first, then put it into \(F = ma\).
Worked example · 5 · Work, energy & powerWorked example

Coefficient of friction by energy

Q (Energy note): A 10 kg box slides 3 m down a rough slope at 30°. Its speed rises from 3.25 m s⁻¹ to 5 m s⁻¹. Find \(\mu\).

  1. B1 PE lost \(= 10(10)(3\sin30°) = 150\ \text{J}\).
  2. B1 KE gained \(= \frac12(10)(5^2 - 3.25^2) = 72.19\ \text{J}\).
  3. M1 Work–energy: WD against friction = PE lost − KE gained \(= 77.81\ \text{J}\).
  4. M1 \(R = 10(10)\cos30° = 86.60\ \text{N}\), so WD \(= \mu(86.60)(3)\).
  5. \(\mu = \dfrac{77.81}{259.8} = 0.300\) A1
Same answer by Newton\(a = \frac{25 - 10.5625}{6} = 2.406\); \(10a = 50 - \mu(86.6)\) ⇒ \(\mu = 0.300\). Pick whichever is fewer lines.

Acceleration and top speed at constant power

Q (MJ2023/41/Q7 a–b): A car of mass 1200 kg on a horizontal road. Engine power constant at 16 kW. Resistance 500 N. (a) Find the acceleration when the speed is 20 m s⁻¹. [3] (b) Find the steady speed. [2]

  1. M1 \(D = \dfrac{P}{v} = \dfrac{16\,000}{20} = 800\ \text{N}\)
  2. M1 \(F = ma\): \(800 - 500 = 1200a\)
  3. \(a = 0.25\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) A1
  4. M1 (b) Steady ⇒ \(a = 0\) ⇒ \(D = 500\): \(\dfrac{16\,000}{v} = 500\)
  5. \(v = 32\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) A1
Part (c) styleOn the hill the resistance varies, so only its work is given. Use work–energy: \(Pt - \text{WD}_{\text{res}} = \Delta KE + mg(d\sin\theta)\).

Exam techniqueStrategy

How to sit Paper 4

  • Timing: 50 marks in 75 min ⇒ 1.5 min per mark. Leave 8 minutes to check signs and units.
  • Diagram first, even when one is printed: add every force, the direction of \(a\), and your positive direction.
  • Name the principle on the line: "N2L for A:", "Resolving ⊥ to the plane:", "CoM:", "Work–energy:". Examiners look for it before awarding M1.
  • One equation per particle in connected systems. Add them to eliminate \(T\).
  • "Show that": answer given, so every step must appear; don't work backwards from it.
  • g = 10 and 3 s.f. unless told otherwise. Exact answers (fractions, surds) are fine if exact.
  • Multi-stage questions (slack strings, braking, bounce): the end speed of one stage is the start speed of the next. Write it down clearly so follow-through marks apply.

Q1 · Forces note[5]

A book of mass 8 kg is at rest on a rough slope at 20° to the horizontal, held in limiting equilibrium by a force of 10 N up the line of greatest slope. (a) Find \(\mu\). (b) Find the largest force up the slope for which the book stays at rest.

Answer(a) Friction acts up the slope: \(10 + \mu(80\cos20°) = 80\sin20°\), so \(\mu = 0.231\). (b) Friction now acts down the slope: \(P = 80\sin20° + 0.231(80\cos20°) = 44.7\ \text{N}\).
Q2 · Newton sheet Q2[6]

A particle of mass 0.8 kg on a rough plane inclined at 28° is held in equilibrium by a force \(T\) N acting at 35° above a line of greatest slope. \(\mu = 0.2\). Find the least and greatest possible values of \(T\).

Answer\(R = 8\cos28° - T\sin35°\). Least (friction up): \(T\cos35° + 0.2R = 8\sin28°\) ⇒ \(T = 3.33\) N. Greatest (friction down): \(T\cos35° = 8\sin28° + 0.2R\) ⇒ \(T = 5.53\) N.
Q3 · Assignment 1 Q4[6]

A car with initial speed \(u\) and constant acceleration \(a\) takes 5 s for the first 80 m and 8 s for the first 160 m. Find \(a\) and \(u\).

Answer\(80 = 5u + 12.5a\), \(160 = 8u + 32a\). Solving: \(a = \frac83 = 2.67\ \text{m s}^{-2}\), \(u = \frac{28}{3} = 9.33\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q4 · Kinematics Quiz 1 Q2(b)(c)[7]

Now \(s = kt^3 + ct^5\). The particle passes P (\(s = 6.25\)) with velocity 1.25 m s⁻¹ at \(t = 5\). Find \(k\) and \(c\), then the acceleration at \(t = 5\).

Answer\(125k + 3125c = 6.25\); \(v = 3kt^2 + 5ct^4\) ⇒ \(75k + 3125c = 1.25\). Subtract: \(k = 0.1\), \(c = -0.002\). \(a = 6kt + 20ct^3 = 3 - 5 = -2\ \text{m s}^{-2}\).

Practice · Newton's laws

Q5 · Newton sheet Q1[6]

A bobsled starts at the top of a 60 m slope inclined at 12°, speed 5 m s⁻¹, and slides straight down. (a) With no resistance, find its speed at the bottom. (b) If instead \(\mu = 0.03\), find the time to reach the bottom.

Answer(a) \(a = 10\sin12° = 2.079\); \(v^2 = 25 + 2(2.079)(60)\) ⇒ \(v = 16.6\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). (b) \(a = 10(\sin12° - 0.03\cos12°) = 1.786\); \(60 = 5t + 0.893t^2\) ⇒ \(t = 5.86\) s.
Q6 · Newton sheet Q3[9]

A van (4500 kg) tows a trailer (750 kg) down a hill, \(\sin\theta = 0.05\), by a light rigid tow-bar. Resistances: van 2500 N, trailer 300 N. (a) Tension is 450 N: find the trailer's acceleration and the van's driving force. (b) Uphill, \(\sin\alpha = 0.09\), driving force 9100 N, speed 20 m s⁻¹ at the bottom: find \(a\), the tension, and the speed after 375 m.

Answer(a) Trailer: \(450 + 375 - 300 = 750a\) ⇒ \(a = 0.7\); van: \(D + 2250 - 2500 - 450 = 4500(0.7)\) ⇒ \(D = 3850\) N. (b) System: \(9100 - 4725 - 2800 = 5250a\) ⇒ \(a = 0.3\); trailer: \(T - 675 - 300 = 225\) ⇒ \(T = 1200\) N; \(v^2 = 400 + 2(0.3)(375)\) ⇒ \(v = 25\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

Practice · Momentum, energy, power

Q7 · Momentum note[3]

A 2 kg particle at 5 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 8 kg particle. Afterwards the 2 kg particle moves at 3 m s⁻¹ in the reverse direction. Find the speed of the 8 kg particle.

Answer\(2(5) + 0 = 2(-3) + 8v\) ⇒ \(v = 2\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q8 · Momentum note[4]

A (0.01 kg), B (0.06 kg), C (0.12 kg) are at rest in a line on a smooth surface, B between. A is given 4 m s⁻¹ towards B, rebounds at 2 m s⁻¹; B then hits C and comes to rest. Find C's speed.

AnswerFirst: \(0.04 = -0.02 + 0.06v_B\) ⇒ \(v_B = 1\). Second: \(0.06(1) = 0.12v_C\) ⇒ \(v_C = 0.5\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q9 · MJ2023/41/Q2[6]

P (0.4 kg) is projected vertically upwards from the ground at 10 m s⁻¹. (a) Find the greatest height. (b) On first hitting the ground it loses 7.2 J and bounces vertically. Find the time between the first and second impacts.

Answer(a) \(0 = 100 - 20h\) ⇒ \(h = 5\) m. (b) KE at impact \(= 20\) J, after \(= 12.8\) J ⇒ \(v = 8\); time up and down \(= 2(8)/10 = 1.6\) s.
Q10 · Energy note[3]

A crate of mass 50 kg slides across a rough horizontal floor from 3 m s⁻¹ to rest in 4 m. Find \(\mu\).

AnswerKE lost \(= 225\) J \(= \mu(500)(4)\) ⇒ \(\mu = 0.1125 = 0.113\).
Q11 · Energy note[4]

A car of mass 800 kg on a level road, resistance 0.5 N per kg, engine at 4 kW. Find (a) the acceleration at 8 m s⁻¹, (b) the maximum speed.

AnswerResistance \(= 400\) N. (a) \(D = 500\); \(500 - 400 = 800a\) ⇒ \(a = 0.125\ \text{m s}^{-2}\). (b) \(4000/v = 400\) ⇒ \(v = 10\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q12 · Momentum note[4]

Carts A (1.2 kg) and B (2.4 kg) move the same way at 9 and 3 m s⁻¹. A collides with B and slows to 1 m s⁻¹. Find B's new velocity and the KE lost.

Answer\(10.8 + 7.2 = 1.2 + 2.4v\) ⇒ \(v = 7\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). KE before \(= 48.6 + 10.8 = 59.4\) J; after \(= 0.6 + 58.8 = 59.4\) J ⇒ no KE lost (a perfectly elastic collision).
