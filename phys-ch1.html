UnitsConcept

Checking an equation with base units

Homogeneous equation

An equation in which every term has the same base units.

Method: write the base units of each term separately. Terms added or subtracted must match; both sides must match.

QuantityDimensionUnit
velocity[L T⁻¹]m s⁻¹
acceleration[L T⁻²]m s⁻²
force[M L T⁻²]kg m s⁻²
Homogeneous ≠ correct\(s = ut + at^2\) is homogeneous but wrong (the ½ is missing). Base units can't check pure numbers.
Spotting a wrong oneFrom your notes: \(s = ut + mg\) fails, because \(mg\) is kg m s⁻², not m.

Scalars and vectorsConcept

Magnitude only, or magnitude and direction?

Scalar

A quantity with magnitude only.

Vector

A quantity with magnitude and direction.

ScalarsVectors
distance, speed, mass, time, temperature, energy, work, power, density, pressure, volumedisplacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight, momentum, torque, electric field
Work and energy are scalarsWork can be negative (force opposite to displacement, \(\theta = 180°\)), but that sign is not a direction.
Pressure is a scalarIt acts in all directions; the force it produces on a surface is the vector.
Scalars and vectorsConcept

Two methods for a resultant

Graphical (scale drawing)Calculation
Draw vectors tip to tail to scale; resultant runs from the first tail to the last tip. Measure its length and angle.Perpendicular: Pythagoras + \(\tan\). Any angle: resolve both into components, add components, recombine.
Resolving

A vector \(F\) at angle \(\theta\) to the x-axis: \(F_x = F\cos\theta\), \(F_y = F\sin\theta\).

Subtracting\(\Delta\vec v = \vec v - \vec u = \vec v + (-\vec u)\): reverse \(\vec u\), then add tip to tail.
Order doesn't matter\(V_1 + V_2 + V_3\) in any order gives the same resultant (your notes check this).

Two kinds of error

Random errorSystematic error
Effectreadings scattered about the true value, above and belowall readings shifted by the same amount (or same proportion) in one direction
Examplesreaction time, fluctuating readings, judging a scale between markszero error, wrongly calibrated meter, parallax always from the same side
Reduce byrepeating and averaging, plotting a graphcorrecting the zero error, recalibrating; averaging does not help
Fix in your notesRandom errors aren't just "human error", and averaging reduces them, it doesn't eliminate them.
Errors and uncertaintiesConcept

Two words examiners test every year

Accuracy

How close a measurement (or the mean) is to the true value. Spoilt by systematic error.

Precision

How close repeated measurements are to each other (small spread). Spoilt by random error.

Fix in your June 16/23 answerYou wrote precision = "the size of the smallest division". That's the instrument's resolution. Precision is the spread of repeated readings about their mean.
Mark-scheme wording (June 16/23 Q2)Systematic: readings all larger (or all smaller) than the true value by the same amount. Random: readings scattered about the true value.

Errors and uncertaintiesConcept

The three rules

OperationRule
\(Q = A \pm B\)add absolute uncertainties: \(\Delta Q = \Delta A + \Delta B\)
\(Q = AB\) or \(A/B\)add fractional (or %) uncertainties: \(\dfrac{\Delta Q}{Q} = \dfrac{\Delta A}{A} + \dfrac{\Delta B}{B}\)
\(Q = A^n\)multiply the fractional uncertainty by \(n\): \(\dfrac{\Delta Q}{Q} = n\dfrac{\Delta A}{A}\)
Never subtract uncertaintiesEven for \(Q = A/B\) or \(Q = A - B\), uncertainties always add.
Final answerAbsolute uncertainty to 1 s.f. (2 if it starts with 1); the value rounded to the same decimal place as the uncertainty.

Electron gunfilament + cathode emit electrons; anodes accelerate and focus them into a beam
Y-platesdeflect the beam vertically by the input p.d.
X-platessweep the beam horizontally at constant speed using a sawtooth p.d. (the time-base)
Screenfluorescent coating glows where the beam hits
voltage = divisions × Y-gain (V div⁻¹) · time = divisions × time-base (s div⁻¹)

\(f = 1/T\). Measure peak-to-peak and halve it for the amplitude, to reduce reading error.

Dual channelTwo traces at once, so two signals can be compared (e.g. phase difference).

Example 1 · Is \(s = ut + \tfrac12at^2\) homogeneous?

Q: Show that the equation \(s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2\) is homogeneous.

  1. B1 LHS: \(s\) has units m
  2. B1 \(ut\): (m s⁻¹)(s) = m
  3. B1 \(\tfrac12 at^2\): (m s⁻²)(s²) = m (½ has no unit)
  4. A1 Every term is m, so the equation is homogeneous
Where marks goShow each term separately. "Both sides are m" without working the RHS terms out scores 0.
Worked examplesWorked example

Example 2 · June 16/21 Q1(b)

Q: A wire has \(d = 0.38 \pm 0.01\) mm, \(L = 25.0 \pm 0.1\) cm and \(M = 0.225 \pm 0.001\) g. Given \(V = \pi d^2L/4\), calculate the density with its absolute uncertainty.

  1. C1 \(V = \dfrac{\pi(0.38\times10^{-3})^2(0.250)}{4} = 2.84\times10^{-8}\ \text{m}^3\)
  2. A1 \(\rho = \dfrac{0.225\times10^{-3}}{2.84\times10^{-8}} = 7.94\times10^{3}\ \text{kg m}^{-3}\)
  3. C1 \(\dfrac{\Delta\rho}{\rho} = 2\left(\dfrac{0.01}{0.38}\right) + \dfrac{0.1}{25.0} + \dfrac{0.001}{0.225} = 0.061\) (6.1%)
  4. A1 \(\Delta\rho = 0.061 \times 7940 \approx 500\), so \(\rho = (7.9 \pm 0.5)\times10^{3}\ \text{kg m}^{-3}\)
Where marks go\(d\) is squared, so its % counts twice. Convert mm and g to m and kg before computing \(\rho\) (your notes stopped at mixed units).

Example 3 · Two forces at 40°

Q: Forces of 8.0 N and 6.0 N act from the same point with 40° between them. Find the resultant.

  1. M1 Components along the 8.0 N: \(R_x = 8.0 + 6.0\cos40° = 12.6\) N; \(R_y = 6.0\sin40° = 3.86\) N
  2. A1 \(R = \sqrt{12.6^2 + 3.86^2} = 13.2\) N
  3. A1 \(\tan\phi = 3.86/12.6 \Rightarrow \phi = 17°\) from the 8.0 N force
Check by scale drawingYour parallelogram gave 13.2 N, which matches. Scale drawings get full marks only if drawn carefully with a stated scale.
MistakesConcept

Where marks are lost

Gram as base unitBase unit of mass is the kilogram.
Prefix not squared1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m², not 10⁻² m².
"Homogeneous so it's correct"Homogeneity can't detect a missing ½ or 2.
Precision = resolutionPrecision is the spread of repeated readings; resolution is the smallest division.
Subtracting uncertaintiesAlways add, whatever the operation.
Uncertainty to 3 s.f.Uncertainty to 1 s.f.; round the value to match its decimal place.
Energy as a vectorEnergy, work, power and pressure are scalars.

Practice · Chapter 1

Q1 · Base units[2]

Use \(V = W/Q\) to express the volt in SI base units.

Answer\(W\): kg m² s⁻²; \(Q = It\): A s. So V = kg m² s⁻² / (A s) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹.
Q2 · Uncertainty[3]

\(I = 2.0 \pm 0.1\) A and \(R = 10.0 \pm 0.2\ \Omega\). Find \(P = I^2R\) with its absolute uncertainty.

Answer\(P = 40\) W. % = 2(5%) + 2% = 12%. \(\Delta P = 4.8 \approx 5\) W, so \(P = 40 \pm 5\) W.
Q3 · Change in velocity[2]

A car's velocity changes from 10 m s⁻¹ east to 10 m s⁻¹ north. Find the change in velocity.

Answer\(\Delta\vec v = \vec v - \vec u\): add 10 north to 10 west. Magnitude \(\sqrt{200} = 14\) m s⁻¹, direction north-west (45° west of north).
Q4 · CRO[3]

A sine wave on a CRO has peak-to-peak height 6.0 div with Y-gain 0.50 V div⁻¹. One cycle covers 5.0 div with time-base 2.0 ms div⁻¹. Find the amplitude and frequency.

AnswerAmplitude = ½ × 6.0 × 0.50 = 1.5 V. \(T = 5.0 \times 2.0 = 10\) ms, so \(f = 100\) Hz.
Q5 · Errors[2]

A student's micrometer reads +0.03 mm with the jaws closed. State the type of error and its effect on the diameters measured, and how to correct it.

AnswerSystematic (zero) error: every reading is 0.03 mm too large. Subtract 0.03 mm from each reading. Averaging would not remove it.
