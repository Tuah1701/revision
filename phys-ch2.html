DefinitionsConcept

Definitions to memorise

Displacement

The distance moved in a specified direction from a fixed point: \(\Delta x = x_f - x_i\).

Speed

The rate of change of distance.

Velocity

The rate of change of displacement.

Acceleration

The rate of change of velocity.

Your notesFrom −2.5 m to −10 m the displacement is −7.5 m; the sign shows direction. Average velocity = total displacement ÷ total time, which can be 0 for a round trip even though average speed isn't.
DefinitionsConcept

Reading one graph off another

  • v at any instant = gradient of the s–t graph (draw a tangent if curved).
  • s–t straight ⇒ constant v; curving up ⇒ speeding up; flat ⇒ at rest; peak ⇒ v = 0 (turning point).
  • a = gradient of v–t. Straight v–t ⇒ constant a.
  • v–t crosses the axis ⇒ object reverses direction.
TrapAt the top of the flight \(v = 0\) but \(a = -9.81\) m s⁻², not zero.
DefinitionsConcept

Ball dropped and bouncing (down +)

  • v–t: straight lines of gradient \(+g\) while falling; at each bounce v jumps from \(+v\) to a smaller \(-v\) almost instantly; then the same gradient \(+g\) again.
  • Each rebound is lower: KE is lost to thermal energy and sound on impact.
  • a–t: constant \(+g\), with large spikes during the short contact times.
  • The steep line at the bounce is the change in momentum over a short contact time ⇒ large force.
State your directionAlways write "down positive" or "up positive" on the axis label. It flips every sign on the v–t graph.
SUVATConcept

The four equations (constant acceleration only)

no \(s\)\(v = u + at\)
no \(a\)\(s = \tfrac12(u + v)t\)
no \(v\)\(s = ut + \tfrac12at^2\)
no \(t\)\(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\)
Derivation (asked for in Paper 2)

\(a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} \Rightarrow v = u + at\)

Area under v–t: \(s = \tfrac12(u+v)t\)

Substitute \(v\): \(s = \tfrac12(2u + at)t = ut + \tfrac12at^2\)

Substitute \(t = (v-u)/a\): \(2as = v^2 - u^2\)

Only for constant aNot valid with air resistance or a changing force.
SUVATConcept

Thinking + braking

stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance
Thinking distancetravelled at constant speed during the reaction time: \(s = ut_r\)
Braking distancewhile decelerating: \(0 = u^2 + 2as \Rightarrow s = \dfrac{u^2}{2|a|}\)
Speed matters twiceThinking distance ∝ \(u\), but braking distance ∝ \(u^2\): doubling speed quadruples braking distance.
Linking to forcesBraking force gives \(a = F/m\). Your notes: 1200 kg car, braking force 2400 N ⇒ \(a = -2\) m s⁻² ⇒ from 20 m s⁻¹, braking distance = 100 m.
Free fallConcept

Acceleration of free fall, g

With no air resistance every object falls with the same acceleration \(g = 9.81\) m s⁻², whatever its mass.

  • Released from rest (down +): \(s = \tfrac12gt^2\), \(v = gt\).
  • s–t is a parabola; v–t a straight line through the origin with gradient \(g\); a–t flat at \(g\).
  • Measuring \(g\): time a fall through measured heights, plot \(s\) against \(t^2\); gradient \(= g/2\).
From class (June 02 Q9)Ball released from rest a distance \(x\) above marker M₁; reaches M₁ at \(t_1\), M₂ (a further \(h\) below) at \(t_2\). Then \(x = \tfrac12at_1^2\) and \(x + h = \tfrac12at_2^2\), so \(a = \dfrac{2h}{t_2^2 - t_1^2}\).
g is not constant at large heightsIt falls with distance from Earth. Near the surface (a few km) treat it as 9.81 m s⁻².
Free fallConcept

Falling with drag

\(a = \dfrac{W - D}{m}\)
  1. At release \(v = 0\), so \(D = 0\) and \(a = g\).
  2. As \(v\) increases, \(D\) increases, so the resultant force and \(a\) decrease.
  3. When \(D = W\): resultant force zero, \(a = 0\), constant terminal velocity.
Heavier objectsSame shape and area but larger mass ⇒ larger W ⇒ drag must be larger to balance ⇒ higher terminal velocity, reached later. Since \(D \propto v^2\), \(v_t \propto \sqrt W\).
ProjectilesConcept

Two independent motions, one shared time

HorizontalVertical
\(u_x = u\cos\theta\), constant\(u_y = u\sin\theta\)
\(a_x = 0\)\(a_y = -g\) (up +)
\(s_x = u_xt\)\(s_y = u_yt - \tfrac12gt^2\)
Horizontal launch\(u_y = 0\): find \(t\) from the drop height, \(t = \sqrt{2h/g}\), then range \(= u_xt\).
ProjectilesConcept

Launched from and landing on level ground

Time of flight\(s_y = 0 \Rightarrow t = \dfrac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\)
Range\(R = u\cos\theta\cdot\dfrac{2u\sin\theta}{g} = \dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}\)
Maximum height\(v_y = 0 \Rightarrow H = \dfrac{(u\sin\theta)^2}{2g}\)

Range is greatest when \(\sin2\theta = 1\), i.e. \(\theta = 45°\).

At the topVelocity is not zero: \(v = u_x\). Only the vertical component is zero.
Velocity after time t (June 03)Vertical \(u\sin\alpha - gt\), horizontal \(u\cos\alpha\). The horizontal part never changes.

Example 1 · Car at 20 m s⁻¹

Q: A driver travelling at 20 m s⁻¹ has a reaction time of 0.20 s. The brakes give a deceleration of 10 m s⁻². Find the stopping distance.

  1. A1 Thinking: \(s = 20 \times 0.20 = 4.0\) m
  2. M1 Braking: \(0 = 20^2 + 2(-10)s\)
  3. A1 \(s = 20\) m
  4. A1 Stopping distance = 4.0 + 20 = 24 m

Example 2 · Ball thrown at a wall

Q: A ball is thrown from S at 15.0 m s⁻¹, 60.0° to the horizontal. The wall is 9.95 m away horizontally. Find (a) the components of the initial velocity, (b) the height at which it hits the wall.

  1. A1 \(u_x = 15.0\cos60° = 7.50\) m s⁻¹
  2. A1 \(u_y = 15.0\sin60° = 13.0\) m s⁻¹
  3. C1 Time to wall: \(t = 9.95/7.50 = 1.33\) s
  4. A1 \(s_y = 13.0(1.33) - \tfrac12(9.81)(1.33)^2 = 8.6\) m
Why it hits at right anglesAt \(t = 1.33\) s, \(v_y = 13.0 - 9.81(1.33) \approx 0\): the ball is at the top of its path, moving horizontally.

Example 3 · Ball thrown down past A

Q: A ball passes A, 5.0 m above the ground, moving down at 8.4 m s⁻¹. Calculate its speed on reaching the ground and show the time taken is 0.47 s.

  1. C1 Down +: \(v^2 = 8.4^2 + 2(9.81)(5.0)\)
  2. A1 \(v = 13\) m s⁻¹
  3. M1 \(v = u + at \Rightarrow 13.0 = 8.4 + 9.81t\)
  4. A1 \(t = 0.47\) s
Sign checkWith down +, \(u = +8.4\) and \(a = +9.81\). Mixing signs is the most common slip here.

Example 4 · Motorcycle stunt

Q: A motorcyclist leaves a ramp horizontally 1.25 m above the ground and lands 10 m away. Find the take-off speed.

Practice · Kinematics

Q1 · Horizontal launch[3]

A tiger leaps horizontally at 3.5 m s⁻¹ from a rock 6.5 m high. How far from the base of the rock does it land?

Answer\(t = \sqrt{2(6.5)/9.81} = 1.15\) s; \(x = 3.5 \times 1.15 = 4.0\) m.
Q2 · SUVAT[2]

A car passes marker posts 100 m apart at 10 m s⁻¹ and then 20 m s⁻¹, accelerating uniformly. Find its acceleration.

Answer\(20^2 = 10^2 + 2a(100) \Rightarrow a = 1.5\) m s⁻².
Q3 · Vertical throw[3]

A ball is thrown vertically up at 12 m s⁻¹. Find its maximum height and its total time in the air (returning to the same level).

Answer\(H = 12^2/(2 \times 9.81) = 7.3\) m; \(t = 2(12)/9.81 = 2.4\) s.
Q4 · Range[2]

A ball is kicked at 20 m s⁻¹. What angle gives the maximum range on level ground, and what is that range?

Answer45°; \(R = u^2/g = 400/9.81 = 41\) m.
Q5 · Rebound[2]

A ball rebounds vertically from the ground at 4.2 m s⁻¹. Find the maximum height it reaches.

Answer\(h = 4.2^2/(2 \times 9.81) = 0.90\) m.
Q6 · Explain[3]

A skydiver jumps from a plane. Explain why she reaches a terminal velocity.

AnswerInitially only weight acts, so a = g. As speed increases, air resistance increases, so the resultant force (W − D) decreases and acceleration decreases. When air resistance equals weight, resultant force is zero, a = 0, and she moves at constant (terminal) velocity.
