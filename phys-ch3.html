Newton's lawsConcept

Definitions

Mass

The property of an object that resists changes in its motion (inertia).

Weight

The gravitational force on a mass: \(W = mg\). It acts at the centre of gravity.

Linear momentum

The product of mass and velocity: \(p = mv\), unit kg m s⁻¹ (= N s). A vector.

Mass ≠ weightMass in kg, a scalar, the same everywhere; weight in N, a vector, depends on g.
Newton's lawsConcept

The three laws in examiner wording

First law

A body remains at rest or moves with constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force.

Second law

The resultant force on a body is proportional to the rate of change of its momentum, and acts in the direction of the change in momentum.

Third law

If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

\(F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{mv - mu}{t}\); constant mass ⇒ \(F = ma\)
Third-law pairsAct on different bodies and are the same type of force. Weight and normal reaction on a book are not a third-law pair (both act on the book).
Newton's lawsConcept

Defining the unit of force

The newton

The force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻² (in the direction of the force).

So 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻². \(F = ma\) is only valid with \(F\) in N, \(m\) in kg, \(a\) in m s⁻².

Always the resultant forceDraw all forces, find the net force along the motion, then \(F_{\text{net}} = ma\).
Impulse and momentumConcept

Force × time = change in momentum

Impulse

The product of force and the time for which it acts: \(F\Delta t = \Delta p\). Unit N s.

Collisions deform objectsDuring impact the large forces squash the ball (your deformed tennis ball sketch). Crumple zones and airbags increase \(\Delta t\), so \(F\) is smaller for the same \(\Delta p\).
ReboundsΔp = m(v − u) with signs: 20 m s⁻¹ in and 15 m s⁻¹ out is a change of 35 m s⁻¹, not 5.
Impulse and momentumConcept

The principle

Principle of conservation of momentum

For a system of interacting bodies, the total momentum remains constant, provided no resultant external force acts.

\(m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2\)
  • Choose a positive direction; velocities the other way are negative.
  • Coalesce (stick together): \(m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = (m_1+m_2)v\).
  • Explosion from rest: total momentum after = 0, so the pieces move in opposite directions.
Why it follows from Newton's lawsThird law: forces on A and B are equal and opposite, for the same time ⇒ equal and opposite impulses ⇒ total Δp = 0.

Elastic collision shortcut

Relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation: \(u_1 - u_2 = v_2 - v_1\).

Impulse and momentumConcept

Deriving \(u_1 + v_1 = u_2 + v_2\)

Two conservation laws

Momentum: \(m_1(u_1 - v_1) = m_2(v_2 - u_2)\) … (1)

KE: \(m_1(u_1^2 - v_1^2) = m_2(v_2^2 - u_2^2)\) … (2)

(2) ÷ (1): \(u_1 + v_1 = v_2 + u_2\)

Rearranged: \(u_1 - u_2 = v_2 - v_1\)

SignsEvery \(u\) and \(v\) carries its sign from your chosen positive direction. N 03 Q12: X right at \(u_X\), Y left at \(u_Y\), separating with X left at \(v_X\), Y right at \(v_Y\): \(u_X + u_Y = v_X + v_Y\).
Impulse and momentumConcept

Conserve each component separately

  • Resolve every momentum into x and y components.
  • Total x-momentum before = total x-momentum after; same for y.
  • If everything starts along x, the total y-momentum after must be zero.
x: \(mu = mv_A\cos60° + mv_B\cos30°\) · y: \(0 = mv_A\sin60° - mv_B\sin30°\)
Not a scalar sumYou can't add momenta in different directions as plain numbers. Resolve first.

Example 1 · Trolleys stick together

Q: A 2.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they move off together. Find the common velocity and the kinetic energy lost. Is the collision elastic?

  1. C1 \(2.0(3.0) + 0 = 3.0v\)
  2. A1 \(v = 2.0\) m s⁻¹
  3. C1 KE before \(= \tfrac12(2.0)(3.0)^2 = 9.0\) J; after \(= \tfrac12(3.0)(2.0)^2 = 6.0\) J
  4. A1 3.0 J lost ⇒ inelastic (KE not conserved)

Example 2 · 2m and m, equal speeds

Q: Masses 2m and m approach head-on, each at speed u, and collide elastically. Show that after the collision 2m moves at u/3 backwards and m at 5u/3.

  1. B1 Right +. Momentum before: \(2mu - mu = mu\)
  2. B1 After: \(2m(-u/3) + m(5u/3) = mu\) ✓
  3. B1 Relative speed of approach \(= u + u = 2u\)
  4. A1 Separation \(= 5u/3 + u/3 = 2u\) ✓ ⇒ elastic
Paper 1 eliminationOption D (stick together) can't be elastic. Test the others for momentum first, then relative speed.

Example 3 · Particles at 60° and 30°

Q: Particle A (mass m) at 500 m s⁻¹ hits stationary B (mass m) elastically. After, A moves at \(v_A\) at 60° to the x-direction and B at \(v_B\) at 30° on the other side. Find \(v_A\) and \(v_B\).

  1. M1 y: \(0 = mv_A\sin60° - mv_B\sin30° \Rightarrow v_B = \sqrt3\,v_A\)
  2. M1 x: \(500m = mv_A\cos60° + mv_B\cos30° = \tfrac12mv_A + \tfrac32mv_A\)
  3. A1 \(v_A = 250\) m s⁻¹
  4. A1 \(v_B = 433\) m s⁻¹
Check elastic\(250^2 + 433^2 = 2.5\times10^5 = 500^2\), so KE is conserved ✓. Initial momentum \(= 1.67\times10^{-27} \times 500 = 8.4\times10^{-25}\) N s.

Example 4 · Ball hits a wall

Q: A 0.15 kg ball hits a wall at 20 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 15 m s⁻¹. Contact time is 0.010 s. Find the impulse and the average force.

  1. C1 Towards wall +: \(\Delta p = 0.15(-15) - 0.15(20)\)
  2. A1 \(\Delta p = -5.25\) N s (magnitude 5.3 N s, away from the wall)
  3. A1 \(F = 5.25/0.010 = 530\) N

Practice · Dynamics

Q1 · Rate of change of momentum[2]

Water leaves a hose at 2.0 kg s⁻¹ and 15 m s⁻¹, hits a wall and stops. Find the force on the wall.

Answer\(F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = 2.0 \times 15 = 30\) N.
Q2 · Elastic collision[4]

A 3.0 kg ball at 4.0 m s⁻¹ collides elastically head-on with a stationary 1.0 kg ball. Find both velocities after.

AnswerMomentum: \(12 = 3v_1 + v_2\). Relative speed: \(4 = v_2 - v_1\). So \(v_1 = 2.0\) m s⁻¹, \(v_2 = 6.0\) m s⁻¹ (both forwards). KE: 24 J before and after ✓.
Q3 · F–t graph[3]

A 0.50 kg ball at rest is struck. The force rises steadily from 0 to 40 N and back to 0 over 0.20 s (triangle). Find the impulse and the ball's final speed.

AnswerImpulse = area = ½(40)(0.20) = 4.0 N s. \(v = 4.0/0.50 = 8.0\) m s⁻¹.
Q4 · Explosion[3]

A 60 kg skater at rest pushes a 40 kg skater, who moves off at 3.0 m s⁻¹. Find the first skater's velocity and explain the direction.

AnswerTotal momentum 0: \(0 = 40(3.0) + 60v \Rightarrow v = -2.0\) m s⁻¹, i.e. 2.0 m s⁻¹ the opposite way. Equal and opposite forces (N3) for the same time give equal and opposite momenta.
Q5 · Define[2]

State Newton's second law and the principle of conservation of momentum.

AnswerN2: resultant force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum (in the direction of the change). Conservation: total momentum of a system is constant provided no resultant external force acts.
