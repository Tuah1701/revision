Displacement–time: the shapes

gradient = velocityarea = nothing useful
TrapA horizontal s–t line means at rest. A horizontal v–t line means constant velocity. Read the y-axis label first, every time.
1 · Kinematics graphsConcept

Instantaneous velocity from a curve

  • Average velocity = chord gradient (total \(\Delta s\) / total \(\Delta t\)).
  • Instantaneous velocity = tangent gradient at that point.
  • Same idea on v–t: tangent gradient = instantaneous acceleration.
Paper 2"Determine" from a graph: show the triangle, read both coordinates, quote the gradient to 2–3 s.f. with unit.

Velocity–time: the shapes

gradient = accelerationarea = displacement

Straight line \(\Rightarrow\) uniform acceleration \(\Rightarrow\) SUVAT is allowed. Curved \(\Rightarrow\) acceleration changing \(\Rightarrow\) no SUVAT; use the graph.

1 · Kinematics graphsConcept

Acceleration–time

\(v = u + \text{(area under a–t)}\). For free fall the a–t graph is a horizontal line at \(g\) (or \(-g\) if up is positive).

1 · Kinematics graphsConcept

Converting between the three graphs

s–tgradient →v–tgradient →a–t
a–tarea →v–tarea →s–t
  • Where v–t has a maximum, a–t crosses zero.
  • Where s–t has a maximum (turning back), v–t crosses zero.
2 · Free fall & projectilesConcept

Dropped from rest, no air resistance

\(v = gt\), \(s = \tfrac12 g t^2\), \(a = g = 9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) for all masses.

2 · Free fall & projectilesConcept

Ball thrown vertically upwards

TrapAt the top \(v = 0\) but \(a = -g\), not zero. The v–t line goes straight through the axis without a kink. Time up = time down (no drag).
2 · Free fall & projectilesConcept

Ball dropped and bouncing

  • Contact time is tiny ⇒ the velocity jump is drawn (almost) vertical.
  • Sign convention matters: with down positive, falling sections have positive v; rising sections negative v.
  • Peak height ratio = KE ratio after/before bounce: \(h_2/h_1 = (v_2/v_1)^2\).
P1 favouriteLook for the graph whose straight segments are all parallel (same gradient g) and whose velocity changes sign at each bounce.
2 · Free fall & projectilesConcept

Projectiles: split into components

HorizontalVertical
Acceleration0\(-g\)
Velocity\(u\cos\theta\) constant\(u\sin\theta - gt\)
Displacement\(x = u\cos\theta\, t\) (linear in t)\(y = u\sin\theta\, t - \tfrac12 g t^2\)

Speed–time for an angled launch: falls to a minimum of \(u\cos\theta\) at the top (not zero), then rises again.

2 · Free fall & projectilesConcept

Linearising: getting g from a graph

  • A positive y-intercept with \(s\) measured from the release point: reaction-time / timing systematic error.
  • Other linear forms: \(v^2\) against \(s\) (gradient \(2g\)); \(v\) against \(t\) (gradient \(g\)).
Paper 3Gradient from a triangle covering more than half the line; quote the uncertainty from the worst acceptable line.
3 · Air resistance & fluidsConcept

Drag and terminal velocity

  • Drag increases with speed and depends on shape/area; acts opposite to velocity.
  • v–t: starts with gradient \(g\), gradient decreases, flattens at \(v_T\).
  • s–t: curve then straight line (constant gradient = \(v_T\)).
3 · Air resistance & fluidsConcept

The 3-mark "explain" chain

  1. As speed increases, air resistance (drag) increases. B1
  2. So the resultant force (\(W - D\)) decreases, so acceleration decreases. B1
  3. When drag = weight, resultant force is zero, acceleration is zero: constant (terminal) velocity. B1
WordingSay "resultant force decreases", not "the force decreases" (weight doesn't change). Never "gravity decreases".
3 · Air resistance & fluidsConcept

Skydiver: two terminal velocities

TrapAfter the parachute opens the skydiver is still moving DOWN but decelerating, so the resultant force is UP. They do not go up.
3 · Air resistance & fluidsConcept

Falling through a liquid

ForceDirectionChanges with speed?
Weight \(W = mg\)downno
Upthrust \(U = \rho_{\text{liq}} g V\)upno
Viscous drag \(D\)opposite to velocityyes, increases

Terminal condition: \(W = U + D\). A ball bearing in oil reaches \(v_T\) within a few cm: why the timing marks in the practical start well below the surface.

4 · Dynamics & momentumConcept

Newton's second law graphs

Definition to quote: the resultant force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum (and in its direction).

4 · Dynamics & momentumConcept

Momentum–time

\(F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\): read two points off the line, divide. A curved p–t needs a tangent.

4 · Dynamics & momentumConcept

Force–time: area = impulse

\(\text{impulse} = \int F\,dt = \Delta p = m(v - u)\). For a triangle: \(\tfrac12 \times F_{\max} \times \Delta t\).

UnitsImpulse in N s; momentum in kg m s⁻¹. They are the same unit.
4 · Dynamics & momentumConcept

Two trolleys colliding

  • During contact the forces on A and B are equal and opposite (N3), for the same time ⇒ equal and opposite changes in momentum.
  • So the p–t graphs of A and B are mirror images; their sum is flat.
  • Ball rebounding off a wall: v–t jumps from \(+u\) to \(-v\); \(\Delta p = m(v + u)\) in magnitude.
5 · Density, pressure, upthrustConcept

Pressure, upthrust and density

\(\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h\) is linear in depth and independent of the container's shape. Upthrust arises because pressure on the bottom face is greater than on the top face.

6 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Work done from F–s

\(W = Fs\cos\theta\). If only part of the force acts along the motion, only that component does work.

6 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Energy against height / distance

ShapesAgainst height or distance: straight lines (\(mgh\), and \(E_k = mgs\) when falling freely). Against time: curves.
6 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Energy against time

Dropped: \(E_k = \tfrac12 m (gt)^2 \propto t^2\). Angled projectile: KE has a minimum of \(\tfrac12 m (u\cos\theta)^2\) at the top, never zero.

6 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Kinetic energy graphs

\(E_k = \tfrac12 mv^2 = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}\). Same momentum, smaller mass ⇒ more KE.

6 · Work, energy & powerConcept

Power: P = Fv

Efficiency \(= \dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}} \times 100\%\): on a Sankey or energy–time graph, compare gradients (powers) or heights (energies).

Worked examplesWorked example

Reading a v–t graph

Q: A car's v–t graph is shown. Find (a) the acceleration in the first 8 s, (b) the deceleration at the end, (c) the total distance, (d) the average speed. [6]

  1. M1 (a) \(a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{20 - 0}{8} = 2.5\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) A1
  2. (b) \(a = \dfrac{0 - 20}{25 - 20} = -4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\), so deceleration \(= 4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) A1
  3. M1 (c) Area: \(\tfrac12(8)(20) + (12)(20) + \tfrac12(5)(20) = 80 + 240 + 50 = 370\ \text{m}\) A1
  4. (d) \(\bar v = \dfrac{370}{25} = 14.8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) A1
Where marks goAverage speed is total distance ÷ total time, NOT \((0 + 20)/2\).
Worked examplesWorked example

Sketching a bouncing-ball v–t graph

Q: A ball is dropped from 1.80 m and rebounds to 0.80 m. Air resistance is negligible. (a) Calculate the speed just before and just after the bounce. (b) Sketch v–t from release to the top of the first rebound, taking downwards as positive. (c) Find the fraction of KE lost. [7]

  1. C1 \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\): \(v = \sqrt{2(9.81)(1.80)} = 5.94\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) down A1
  2. Up to 0.80 m: \(v = \sqrt{2(9.81)(0.80)} = 3.96\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) up A1
  3. Times: fall \(t = 5.94/9.81 = 0.61\ \text{s}\); rise \(t = 3.96/9.81 = 0.40\ \text{s}\), so top at 1.01 s.
  4. B1B1 Sketch: straight line 0 → +5.94 at 0.61 s; vertical drop to −3.96; straight line back to 0 at 1.01 s, parallel to the first.
  5. C1 KE ∝ h (all KE becomes GPE): fraction lost \(= 1 - \dfrac{0.80}{1.80} = 0.56\) A1
Worked examplesWorked example

Area under F–t gives Δp

Q: A ball of mass 0.060 kg moving at 20 m s⁻¹ hits a wall normally. The force on it varies as shown. Find (a) the impulse, (b) the velocity after the impact, (c) whether the collision is elastic. [5]

  1. C1 (a) Impulse = area \(= \tfrac12 (600)(8.0\times10^{-3}) = 2.4\ \text{N s}\) A1
  2. Take away from the wall as positive: \(u = -20\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
  3. C1 (b) \(2.4 = 0.060(v - (-20))\Rightarrow v + 20 = 40 \Rightarrow v = 20\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) away from the wall A1
  4. (c) Speed unchanged ⇒ KE unchanged ⇒ elastic. B1
TrapIgnoring the sign of \(u\) gives \(2.4 = 0.060(v - 20)\Rightarrow v = 60\ \text{m s}^{-1}\): impossible (more KE than it arrived with). For a rebound, \(|\Delta p| = m(v + u)\).
Worked examplesWorked example

Driving force against speed

Q: A car of mass 1200 kg has a constant output power of 60 kW. Resistive forces are a constant 2000 N. Find (a) the maximum speed, (b) the acceleration at 15 m s⁻¹. [5]

  1. C1 (a) Max speed: \(a = 0\) so driving force \(= 2000\ \text{N}\). \(v = \dfrac{P}{F} = \dfrac{60\,000}{2000} = 30\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) A1
  2. C1 (b) At 15 m s⁻¹: \(F = \dfrac{60\,000}{15} = 4000\ \text{N}\)
  3. C1 \(F_{\text{res}} = 4000 - 2000 = 2000\ \text{N}\)
  4. \(a = \dfrac{2000}{1200} = 1.7\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) A1
Graph readingMax speed is where the \(P/v\) curve meets the resistance line.
ExamCommon mistakes

Where graph marks are lost

  • Wrong graph read: treating v–t as s–t. Check the y-axis label first.
  • Gradient from data points not on the line: use points read from the best-fit line, large triangle.
  • Prefixes on axes: t in ms or F in kN: convert to s and N before finding the area or gradient (factor of 1000 errors).
  • a = 0 at the top of a vertical throw: wrong, a = −g throughout.
  • Bounce sketch: segments not parallel, or velocity doesn't change sign, or rebound speed too big.
  • Terminal velocity: "forces balance so it stops". No: zero resultant force = constant velocity, not rest.
  • Parachute opening: drawing v going negative. It still moves down, just more slowly.
  • Projectile: horizontal velocity drawn changing (no drag) or speed at the top drawn as zero.
  • Energy vs height drawn curved: it is linear. Energy vs time is curved.
  • Displacement vs distance when v–t crosses the axis.

Practice · Kinematics & free fall

Q1 · sketch[3]

A ball is thrown vertically upwards and caught at the same height. Taking upwards as positive and ignoring air resistance, sketch (a) displacement–time, (b) velocity–time, (c) acceleration–time.

Answer(a) Inverted parabola from 0, max at mid-time with zero gradient, back to 0. (b) Straight line from \(+u\) to \(-u\), crossing zero at mid-time, gradient \(-g\). (c) Horizontal line at \(-9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) throughout.
Q2 · v–t[4]

A cyclist accelerates uniformly from 4.0 m s⁻¹ to 10 m s⁻¹ in 3.0 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 2.0 s. Sketch the v–t graph and find the total distance and the deceleration.

AnswerDistance \(= \tfrac12(4+10)(3) + \tfrac12(10)(2) = 21 + 10 = 31\ \text{m}\). Deceleration \(= 10/2.0 = 5.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\).
Q3 · projectile[6]

A stone is thrown horizontally at 12 m s⁻¹ from a cliff 20 m high. Sketch horizontal and vertical velocity against time. Find the time of flight, the horizontal distance and the speed on impact.

Answer\(v_x\): horizontal line at 12. \(v_y\) (down +): straight line from 0, gradient 9.81. \(t = \sqrt{2(20)/9.81} = 2.02\ \text{s}\); \(x = 12 \times 2.02 = 24\ \text{m}\); \(v_y = 19.8\), speed \(= \sqrt{12^2 + 19.8^2} = 23\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q4 · Paper 3 style[4]

A student plots distance fallen \(s\) against \(t^2\) and gets a straight line through the origin with gradient 4.9 m s⁻². Determine \(g\), and state what a positive \(t^2\)-intercept would suggest.

Answer\(s = \tfrac12 g t^2\), gradient \(= g/2\) ⇒ \(g = 9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}\). A \(t^2\)-intercept means measured times are systematically too long (e.g. reaction time in starting/stopping the stopwatch, or release delay).

Practice · Drag & momentum

Q5 · explain[4]

A steel ball is released from rest just below the surface of oil. Sketch its v–t graph and explain its shape in terms of the forces acting.

AnswerCurve from 0, initial gradient less than g (upthrust), flattening to \(v_T\). Weight and upthrust constant; viscous drag increases with speed, so resultant force \(W - U - D\) and acceleration decrease; when \(W = U + D\) resultant force is zero and velocity is constant.
Q6 · p–t[3]

A trolley's momentum increases linearly from 2.0 N s to 8.0 N s in 3.0 s. Its mass is 0.50 kg. Find the resultant force and the change in velocity.

Answer\(F = \Delta p/\Delta t = 6.0/3.0 = 2.0\ \text{N}\). \(\Delta v = \Delta p/m = 6.0/0.50 = 12\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q7 · F–t[3]

The resultant force on a 2.0 kg trolley, initially at rest, rises linearly from 0 to 6.0 N in 2.0 s, then stays at 6.0 N for a further 3.0 s. Find its speed at 5.0 s.

AnswerImpulse \(= \tfrac12(6.0)(2.0) + 6.0(3.0) = 6.0 + 18 = 24\ \text{N s}\). \(v = 24/2.0 = 12\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q8 · collision[5]

Trolley A (2.0 kg, 3.0 m s⁻¹) hits stationary trolley B (1.0 kg) and they stick. Sketch momentum–time for A, B and the total, and find the KE lost.

Answer\(6.0 = 3.0v\) ⇒ \(v = 2.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). \(p_A\): 6.0 → 4.0; \(p_B\): 0 → 2.0 (mirror image); total flat at 6.0 N s. KE: \(9.0 - \tfrac12(3.0)(2.0)^2 = 9.0 - 6.0 = 3.0\ \text{J}\) lost.
Practice · 9–10Practice

Practice · Pressure, work & energy

Q9 · energy[5]

A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. (a) Sketch KE and GPE against height with no air resistance. (b) With a constant air resistance of 1.0 N, find the KE and speed at the ground.

Answer(a) Two straight lines: GPE from 49 J at 10 m to 0; KE from 0 at 10 m to 49 J at ground; total flat at 49 J. (b) \(E_k = mgh - Fs = 49.05 - 10 = 39\ \text{J}\); \(v = \sqrt{2(39.05)/0.50} = 12.5\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).
Q10 · F–s and p–h[5]

(a) A force rises linearly from 0 to 40 N over 0.20 m, then stays at 40 N for a further 0.30 m. Find the work done. (b) A total pressure–depth graph for a liquid has intercept \(1.0\times10^5\) Pa and reads \(1.49\times10^5\) Pa at 5.0 m. Find the density.

Answer(a) \(W = \tfrac12(40)(0.20) + 40(0.30) = 4.0 + 12 = 16\ \text{J}\). (b) \(\rho = \dfrac{\Delta p}{g\Delta h} = \dfrac{4.9\times10^4}{9.81 \times 5.0} = 1.0\times10^3\ \text{kg m}^{-3}\).
