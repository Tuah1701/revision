MomentsConcept

One force standing in for many

Every particle of an object is pulled by gravity. All these forces can be replaced by one force, the weight, acting at a single point.

Centre of gravity

The point at which the whole weight of an object may be considered to act.

Uniform objectsFor a uniform rod or metre rule, the centre of gravity is at the midpoint (50 cm mark).
MomentsConcept

The turning effect of a force

Moment of a force

The product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.

Depends on two things: the magnitude of the force and the perpendicular distance. Unit N m.

Angled forceEither use the perpendicular component (\(F\sin\theta \times d\)) or the perpendicular distance (\(F \times d\sin\theta\)). They give the same answer.
MomentsConcept

Balancing turning effects

Principle of moments

For an object in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about the same point.

  • Take moments about a point where an unknown force acts: it has zero moment there and drops out.
  • Include the object's own weight at its centre of gravity.
  • Any point works if the object is in equilibrium. Pick the one that removes the most unknowns.
Ladder against a smooth wall (June 08)Moments about the base: the wall's push \(F\) at height \(h\) balances the weight \(W\) at horizontal distance \(a\): \(Fh = Wa\). About the top, with friction at the base: \(Wa + Fh = 2Wa\).
MomentsConcept

A pair of forces that only turns

Couple

A pair of forces, equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, acting along parallel lines (not the same line), producing rotation only.

Torque of a couple

The product of one of the forces and the perpendicular distance between the forces.

Same about any pointYour notes: 6 N forces 3 m apart. About P (1 m from one): 6(1) + 6(2) = 18 N m. About Q (on one force): 6(3) = 18 N m. The torque is independent of the axis.
Couple vs single momentA couple has zero resultant force, so no linear acceleration. A single force off-centre gives both turning and translation.

Translational motion

Every part moves in the same direction at the same speed.

Rotational motion

Every part moves in a circle about an axis.

Three forces in equilibriumTheir lines of action pass through one point, and they form a closed triangle when drawn tip to tail.
Ladder on icy ground (June 12/12)No friction at the base, so the ground's force R is vertical; the rough wall provides both a horizontal push and an upward friction force.
Density and pressureConcept

Definitions

Density

Mass per unit volume: \(\rho = \dfrac mV\), unit kg m⁻³.

Pressure

Normal force per unit area: \(p = \dfrac FA\), unit Pa (N m⁻²).

Pressure in a fluid

Column of height \(h\), area \(A\): \(m = \rho V = \rho Ah\)

\(p = \dfrac{mg}{A} = \dfrac{\rho Ahg}{A} = \rho gh\)

Total pressureUnder a liquid open to the air: \(p = p_{\text{atm}} + \rho gh\). Higher altitude ⇒ less air above ⇒ lower atmospheric pressure.
Density and pressureConcept

Why things float

Upthrust

The upward force exerted by a fluid on a submerged object, caused by the difference in pressure between its bottom and top surfaces.

Derivation

Top at depth \(h_1\): \(F_1 = \rho gh_1A\) (down)

Bottom at depth \(h_2\): \(F_2 = \rho gh_2A\) (up)

Upthrust \(= F_2 - F_1 = \rho gA(h_2 - h_1) = \rho_f gV\)

\(\rho_f V g = m_f g\): upthrust = weight of fluid displaced (Archimedes' principle).

FloatingUpthrust = weight. A floating block of density 600 kg m⁻³ in water (1000) sits with 60% of its volume submerged.

Example 1 · Balancing a metre rule

Q: A uniform 100 g metre rule is pivoted at the 40 cm mark. A string at the 100 cm mark runs over a pulley to a 20 g mass, pulling the rule upwards. Where must a 50 g mass hang for the rule to balance?

  1. B1 Moments about the pivot (g cancels, use grams and cm)
  2. C1 Clockwise: rule's weight \(100 \times (50-40) = 1000\); anticlockwise: tension \(20 \times (100-40) = 1200\)
  3. M1 Need 200 more clockwise: \(50 \times x = 200 \Rightarrow x = 4\) cm right of the pivot
  4. A1 44 cm mark
Your workingYou mixed metres and newtons with signs and got lost. Keep one consistent unit (g and cm is fine here, since g cancels) and list clockwise and anticlockwise separately.

Example 2 · Steering wheel and lever (June 14/11)

Q (a): A driver pushes with 15 N on opposite sides of a wheel of diameter 0.40 m. Find the torque.

Q (b): A 1.20 m lever turns a disc of radius 0.20 m, which winds a string lifting a 900 N weight. Equal forces F act at each end of the lever. Find the minimum F.

  1. A1 (a) \(\tau = 15 \times 0.40 = 6.0\) N m
  2. M1 (b) Couple torque = load torque: \(F(1.20) = 900(0.20)\)
  3. A1 \(F = 150\) N

Example 3 · Person on a plank

Q: A uniform 4.0 m plank of weight 200 N rests on supports at each end. A 500 N person stands 1.0 m from the left end. Find both support forces.

  1. M1 Moments about the left support: \(R_R(4.0) = 200(2.0) + 500(1.0)\)
  2. A1 \(R_R = 225\) N
  3. A1 Vertical forces: \(R_L = 700 - 225 = 475\) N
Sense checkThe person is nearer the left, so the left support carries more.

Example 4 · Object in water

Q: An object of volume \(2.0\times10^{-4}\) m³ weighs 5.0 N in air. Find the upthrust when fully submerged in water (1000 kg m⁻³), its apparent weight, and the pressure due to the water at 12 m depth.

  1. A1 \(U = \rho gV = 1000(9.81)(2.0\times10^{-4}) = 1.96\) N
  2. A1 Apparent weight \(= 5.0 - 1.96 = 3.0\) N
  3. A1 \(\Delta p = \rho gh = 1000(9.81)(12) = 1.2\times10^5\) Pa

Practice · Forces, density and pressure

Q1 · Define[2]

Define the moment of a force and the torque of a couple.

AnswerMoment: force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to its line of action. Torque of a couple: one force × perpendicular distance between the two forces.
Q2 · Principle of moments[3]

A uniform 2.0 m rod of weight 30 N is pivoted at its centre. A 40 N weight hangs 0.60 m left of the pivot. Where must a 60 N weight hang to balance it?

AnswerRod's weight acts at the pivot (no moment). \(40(0.60) = 60x \Rightarrow x = 0.40\) m right of the pivot.
Q3 · Pressure[2]

Find the pressure due to 0.80 m of oil (density 850 kg m⁻³), and the total pressure if atmospheric pressure is \(1.01\times10^5\) Pa.

Answer\(\rho gh = 850(9.81)(0.80) = 6.7\times10^3\) Pa; total \(= 1.08\times10^5\) Pa.
Q4 · Floating[2]

A wooden cube of side 0.10 m and density 600 kg m⁻³ floats in water. How deep does it sit?

AnswerUpthrust = weight: \(1000 \times 0.10^2 \times h = 600 \times 0.10^3 \Rightarrow h = 0.060\) m.
Q5 · Explain[2]

Explain why a submerged object experiences an upthrust.

AnswerPressure in a fluid increases with depth, so the pressure on the bottom surface is greater than on the top. This gives a greater upward force than downward force, so a resultant upward force.
