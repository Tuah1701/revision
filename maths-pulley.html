StartOverview

The string pulls the pulley twice

Smooth, light pulley + light, inextensible string ⇒ the tension \(T\) is the same on both sides. Each segment pulls the pulley along itself, away from the pulley. The force on the pulley is the vector sum of the two tensions.

\(R = 2T\cos\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}\right)\), along the bisector of θ
WhyResolve along the bisector: each tension gives \(T\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\). Across the bisector the two components are equal and opposite, so they cancel.
StartConcept

Every pulley-force question in two steps

1 · N2L on one particle ⇒ \(T\)→2 · Geometry ⇒ θ, then \(R = 2T\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\) + direction
  • Step 1 first, always. \(T\) equals the hanging weight only in equilibrium. If the system accelerates, \(T \neq mg\).
  • Get \(a\) from the whole system (external forces ÷ total mass), then \(T\) from one particle on its own.
  • Step 2: find θ from the diagram. If unsure of θ, resolve horizontally and vertically instead and use Pythagoras.
The supportThe clamp/axle holding the pulley exerts a force equal and opposite to \(R\). "Force exerted by the string on the pulley" and "force needed to hold the pulley" have the same magnitude.

Case 1 · Both strings hanging vertically

Angle between strings\(\theta = 0°\)
Magnitude\(R = 2T\cos 0° = 2T\)
DirectionVertically downwards
Trap\(R = 2T\), not the total weight. With 3 kg and 5 kg released, \(T = 37.5\) N, so \(R = 75\) N, not 80 N.
The four casesConcept

Case 2 · Pulley at the edge of a horizontal table

Angle between strings\(\theta = 90°\)
Magnitude\(R = 2T\cos45°\)
\(\phantom{R} = T\sqrt2\)
Direction\(45°\) below the horizontal, towards the table (bisects the right angle)
Rough table?Friction changes \(a\) and so \(T\), but not the geometry: still \(R = T\sqrt2\) at \(45°\).
The four casesConcept

Case 3 · Pulley at the top of an inclined plane

Angle between strings\(\theta = 90° - \alpha\)
Magnitude\(R = 2T\cos\left(45° - \frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\)
\(\phantom{R} = T\sqrt{2(1+\sin\alpha)}\)
DirectionAt \(45° - \frac{\alpha}{2}\) to the vertical, on the slope side
Trapθ is not the slope angle. On a \(20°\) slope, \(\theta = 70°\).
The four casesConcept

Check Case 3 without the formula

One string runs down the slope at α below the horizontal; the other hangs vertically.

Resolve the two tensions

\(R_x = T\cos\alpha\)

\(R_y = T + T\sin\alpha\)

\(R^2 = T^2\cos^2\alpha + T^2(1+\sin\alpha)^2\)

\(\phantom{R^2} = T^2(2 + 2\sin\alpha)\)

\(R = T\sqrt{2(1 + \sin\alpha)}\)

Same as \(2T\cos\left(45° - \frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\), because

\(4\cos^2\left(45° - \frac{\alpha}{2}\right) = 2\left(1 + \sin\alpha\right)\)

Exam fallbackResolving always works for any geometry. If you're not sure what θ is, resolve, then \(\tan\phi = R_y / R_x\) gives the direction.
The four casesConcept

Case 4 · Pulley at the apex of a wedge

Angle between strings\(\theta = 180° - \alpha - \beta\)
Magnitude\(R = 2T\cos\left(90° - \frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\right)\)
\(\phantom{R} = 2T\sin\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\right)\)
DirectionBisects the angle between the faces, into the wedge
Special case\(\alpha + \beta = 90°\) ⇒ \(\theta = 90°\) ⇒ \(R = T\sqrt2\), like Case 2.

Example 1 · 3 kg and 5 kg over a pulley

Q: Particles of mass 3 kg and 5 kg hang on either side of a fixed smooth pulley and are released from rest. Find the force exerted on the pulley by the string.

  1. Directions: 5 kg down positive, 3 kg up positive.
  2. M1 N2L (5 kg): \(50 - T = 5a\); N2L (3 kg): \(T - 30 = 3a\)
  3. A1 Add: \(20 = 8a \Rightarrow a = 2.5\ \text{m s}^{-2}\), so \(T = 30 + 3(2.5) = 37.5\ \text{N}\)
  4. M1 Case 1, \(\theta = 0°\): \(R = 2T\)
  5. A1 \(R = 75\ \text{N}\), vertically downwards
Where marks goWriting \(R = 80\) N (total weight) scores 0. The system accelerates, so \(T < 50\) N.

Example 2 · Block on a table, particle hanging

Q: A 4 kg block on a smooth horizontal table is connected over a smooth pulley at the edge to a 1 kg hanging particle. Find the magnitude and direction of the force on the pulley.

  1. M1 System (external forces only): \(10 = (4+1)a \Rightarrow a = 2\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)
  2. A1 N2L (4 kg block): \(T = 4a = 8\ \text{N}\)
  3. M1 Case 2, \(\theta = 90°\): \(R = T\sqrt2\)
  4. A1 \(R = 8\sqrt2 = 11.3\ \text{N}\) at \(45°\) below the horizontal
Check\(T = 8 < 10\) N (hanging weight), as it must be while the 1 kg particle accelerates down.

Example 3 · KYUEM Trial 2023 Q4 extension

Q: Block B (2 kg) is on a smooth plane inclined at \(20°\), connected over a pulley at the top to ball A (3 kg) hanging freely. Find the force on the pulley while A falls.

  1. M1 System: \(30 - 20\sin20° = 5a \Rightarrow a = 4.632\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)
  2. A1 N2L (A, down +): \(30 - T = 3a \Rightarrow T = 16.10\ \text{N}\)
  3. B1 Case 3: \(\theta = 90° - 20° = 70°\)
  4. M1 \(R = 2(16.10)\cos35°\)
  5. A1 \(R = 26.4\ \text{N}\) at \(35°\) to the vertical, on the slope side
Check by resolving\(R_x = 16.10\cos20° = 15.13\), \(R_y = 16.10(1 + \sin20°) = 21.61\), so \(R = \sqrt{15.13^2 + 21.61^2} = 26.4\) N ✓

Where marks are lost

Using \(mg\) as \(T\)Only true in equilibrium. If anything accelerates, find \(T\) with N2L first.
Slope angle as θθ is the angle between the strings. On a \(20°\) incline, \(\theta = 70°\).
Different tensions each sideSmooth light pulley ⇒ one \(T\) throughout the string.
No direction"Find the force on the pulley" usually means magnitude and direction. State the angle and the reference line.
\(T\) pulling towards the pulleyOn the pulley, each tension points away from it, along the string towards the particle.

Practice · Force on a pulley

Q1 · Case 1[4]

Particles of mass 6 kg and 4 kg hang on either side of a fixed smooth pulley and are released from rest. Find the force exerted on the pulley by the string.

Answer\(20 = 10a \Rightarrow a = 2\); \(T = 40 + 4(2) = 48\) N; \(R = 2T = 96\) N vertically downwards.
Q2 · Case 2, rough table[5]

A 5 kg block on a rough horizontal table (\(\mu = 0.2\)) is connected over a pulley at the edge to a 3 kg hanging particle. Find the magnitude and direction of the force on the pulley.

Answer\(30 - 0.2(50) = 8a \Rightarrow a = 2.5\); \(T = 3(10 - 2.5) = 22.5\) N; \(R = 22.5\sqrt2 = 31.8\) N at \(45°\) below the horizontal.
Q3 · Case 4, wedge[6]

A wedge has smooth faces inclined at \(30°\) and \(60°\). A 4 kg particle on the \(30°\) face and a 6 kg particle on the \(60°\) face are connected over a pulley at the apex. Find the force on the pulley.

Answer\(60\sin60° - 40\sin30° = 10a \Rightarrow a = 3.196\); \(T = 20 + 4a = 32.8\) N; \(\theta = 90°\), \(R = 2T\sin45° = 46.4\) N, at \(15°\) to the vertical towards the \(30°\) face.
Q4 · Case 3, equilibrium[4]

A 5 kg block rests on a smooth plane inclined at \(30°\), held in equilibrium by a string over a pulley at the top attached to a hanging particle of mass \(m\) kg. Find \(m\) and the force on the pulley.

AnswerEquilibrium: \(T = 50\sin30° = 25\) N, so \(m = 2.5\). \(\theta = 60°\): \(R = 2(25)\cos30° = 43.3\) N at \(30°\) to the vertical.
